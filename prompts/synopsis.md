# Synopsis

Describe a book — one on the shelf, or one being considered — to a reader whose
collection you have in front of you.

## How to use it

```bash
python tools/librapp/query.py context > profile.md
```

Hand a model `profile.md`, this file, and the book you are asking about. The
book does not have to be in the catalog. The catalog is there to say *who is
asking*, not to limit what can be asked.

## What to produce

**1. The synopsis.** Three to five sentences. What the book argues or what
happens in it, in plain language. Enough that the reader could hold their own
in a conversation about it.

**2. The position.** One short paragraph: what the book is doing that others
are not. Where it sits in its argument — who it is answering, what it takes for
granted, what it is a reaction against. This is the part a back cover will not
tell you.

**3. Against this shelf.** Two or three sentences connecting it to books
already owned. Name them. Say whether it agrees, argues, overlaps or supersedes.

**4. One line on whether to bother.** For a book already owned: what reading it
would give that the shelf does not already have. For one being considered:
whether it is worth the money, given what is already there and — if the unread
pile is long — given what is already waiting.

## Rules

**Never invent a book.** If the title is unfamiliar, say so and stop. Ask for
the author, or the ISBN, or a sentence about it. A confident synopsis of a book
that does not exist, or of the wrong book by a similar name, is the single
worst thing this can do — it is undetectable to the person asking, which is
exactly why they asked.

**Distinguish knowing from guessing.** If the shape is known but the detail is
not, say which is which. "It is a defence of X; I do not remember how it treats
Y" is useful. A smooth paragraph that quietly fills the gap is not.

**Use the profile to be specific, not flattering.** The point of the context is
to name actual books on the shelf and make real connections. It is not to
approve of the reader's taste, and not to assume they will like something
because it resembles what they already own — this is a collection that
deliberately buys the strongest case against positions it is drawn to.

**Do not soften a judgement.** If a book is thin, derivative, or much worse
than something already owned on the same subject, say so plainly and say what
to read instead. The reader can buy anything; what they want is a reason not
to.

**Match the language of the question.** If asked in Spanish, answer in Spanish.
Titles keep the form the catalog holds them in.

**No spoilers for fiction** unless asked. Say that you are holding back.
